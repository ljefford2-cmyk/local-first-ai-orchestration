## CR-1.0 — Cross-Volume Canonicalization Rule

### Purpose

Organize the 239 frozen Track B source rows into auditable cross-volume canonical groups while preserving every original requirement ID, controlling passage, actor, condition, scope, and member-specific obligation.

Canonicalization identifies relationships among source rows. It does not create new requirements.

### Unit of canonicalization

The indivisible input unit is one complete source requirement row.

A source row may belong to exactly one canonical row.

Do not merge only one clause of a source row while leaving another clause outside the group. If the complete operative scope of a source row does not align with the proposed group, keep it distinct or record an unresolved relationship.

### Original-ID preservation

Every original ID remains permanent:

- `V1-R001–V1-R058`
- `V2-R001–V2-R087`
- `V3-R001–V3-R094`

No original ID is retired, replaced, or made subordinate to a canonical ID.

Every source ID must occur exactly once as a canonical member.

### Canonical-row standing

A canonical row is a non-controlling index and crosswalk.

Normative force remains in:

1. the controlling manuscript passages;
2. the original extracted source rows.

A canonical-core statement is a non-controlling synopsis of common operative content. It may not replace the member passages in later assurance work.

Do not say:

> The architecture has N requirements.

Use:

> CR-1.0 organized 239 frozen Track B source rows into N non-controlling canonical rows.

### Comparison dimensions

Before classifying two rows, compare all materially relevant dimensions:

1. operative proposition;
2. actor or bound function;
3. governed object;
4. organizational claim or authority affected;
5. required function or evidence property;
6. scope;
7. triggering condition;
8. consequence class or operating condition;
9. lifecycle or temporal phase;
10. direction of the requirement;
11. exceptions, limitations, or qualifications;
12. declaration, evaluation, or integration role;
13. operative consequence if violated.

A shared topic, controlled term, section subject, or vocabulary word is not sufficient for merger.

A difference in extraction tier does not by itself require separation and does not justify merger. Tiers describe how force was expressed in the source passage; they are not canonical severity levels.

Do not assign one canonical tier. Preserve every member’s original tier.

### Relationship 1 — Exact duplicate

Classify two source rows as exact duplicates only where they establish the same operative requirement with materially the same:

- actor or bound function;
- governed object;
- claim or authority;
- scope;
- triggering condition;
- lifecycle phase;
- operative consequence;
- limitation or exception.

No meaningful obligation, prohibition, condition, or evidence property may be lost by treating the passages as coextensive.

Logical similarity is insufficient.

Do not use a converse, contrapositive, generalized principle, inferred purpose, or broad architectural theme to manufacture equivalence.

### Relationship 2 — Reinforcing expression

Classify a source row as a reinforcing expression where:

1. it establishes the same core operative requirement;
2. actor, governed function, claim, and triggering condition remain materially aligned;
3. it adds explicit, non-conflicting scope, detail, evidence properties, limitations, or implementation-neutral precision;
4. the additional content is preserved visibly rather than flattened into the common core.

A reinforcing group must contain:

- a minimum common operative core;
- every member ID;
- every member controlling passage;
- every member-specific retained addition.

The canonical row does not transform the additions into optional commentary. They remain source-established obligations attached to their original IDs.

### Relationship 3 — Related but distinct

Keep rows in separate canonical rows where they concern a similar subject but differ materially in any of the following:

- actor;
- claim;
- authority;
- governed object;
- function;
- trigger;
- scope;
- lifecycle phase;
- evidence burden;
- consequence;
- direction of responsibility;
- definition versus evaluation versus integration role.

Examples of distinctions that must not be flattened:

- defining an organizational object versus evaluating that object;
- evaluating evidence versus constructing an evidence path;
- defining authority versus verifying exercise of authority;
- declaring a boundary versus observing whether operation stayed inside it;
- qualifying capability versus authorizing use;
- authorizing action versus proving effect;
- a general prohibition versus a separately triggered special case.

Related-but-distinct rows may be cross-linked, but they are not members of the same canonical row.

### Uncertainty rule

No silent merging.

If the relationship cannot be confidently classified from the source rows and permitted manuscript context:

1. keep the rows in separate canonical rows;
2. record the relationship as unresolved;
3. state exactly which comparison dimension remains unclear;
4. request owner adjudication.

A false split is reversible.
A false merge destroys distinctions.

### Pairwise compatibility

Do not assume relationship transitivity.

If a proposed group contains more than two members, verify that every member is compatible with the complete group.

If any member is related-but-distinct from another member, split the group and create cross-links.

### Ordering and canonical IDs

Use this fixed source order:

1. `V1-R001–V1-R058`
2. `V2-R001–V2-R087`
3. `V3-R001–V3-R094`

Iterate in that order.

The earliest unassigned source ID becomes the ordering anchor for the next canonical row. The ordering anchor has no greater normative authority than any other member.

Assign canonical IDs sequentially:

- `B1.4-C001`
- `B1.4-C002`
- …

No gaps.
