B1.1 — Volume 1 Requirement Extraction
Extraction Rule Version: ER-1.0 (2026-08-22)
Record this version string in the completion footer. If any part of this rule is changed before B1.2 or B1.3 run, those passes must record their own version, and canonicalization must treat registers built under different versions as built to different standards.
Purpose
Extract the operational requirements the controlling architecture actually establishes, from Volume 1 — Define Reality, Final Controlled Manuscript only.
This pass produces a denominator. It does not assess anything.
This is Track B, pass B1.1. It is read-only with respect to the corpus and produces two new artifacts.
Hard boundaries
Do not:

* map evidence, tests, fixtures, or implementation against any requirement;
* consult the federal fixture, the harness, the gateway, STATUS.md, or any test suite;
* consult Volumes 2 or 3, the Project Plan, the Design Bases, the Structural Outlines, the brownfield paper, or the reconciliation matrix;
* canonicalize, deduplicate across volumes, or merge with anything;
* assess whether a requirement is satisfied, satisfiable, implemented, or reasonable;
* propose remediation, tests, allocation, or architecture;
* modify Volume 1 or any controlling artifact.

The reason for the isolation: requirement extraction must finish before evidence is in view. If you know a requirement has nothing behind it, the temptation is to reclassify the passage as explanatory. Extract as though no implementation exists and none is planned.
Within Volume 1, extract from the entire Final Controlled Manuscript, beginning through end — including appendices, tables, records, and closing sections.
Inclusion test
Include a requirement when the controlling text establishes a condition, constraint, obligation, prohibition, required distinction, required evidence property, or required governing function whose satisfaction could materially change whether an AI-mediated operation, capability, assurance claim, or consequence is defensible under the architecture.
Apply this test through the three tiers below.
Tier 1 — Explicit enumerated requirements
Always include: named requirements; required records and their stated contents; required functions and interfaces; stated invariants; defined claim states; capability results; baseline requirements; required evidence properties; explicit must / shall / required / may-not conditions.
Tier 2 — Operative constraints expressed without requirement keywords
Include statements whose violation changes what may be claimed or permitted, even where no requirement keyword appears. Examples of the form: evidence places an upper bound on what may be defensibly claimed; repetition does not create authority; a stated non-conversion between capability and permission.
Mandatory field for every Tier 2 row:
Operative consequence if violated: one sentence stating what claim, authority, assurance state, boundary, or permitted consequence would change.
If that field cannot be completed from the controlling text without inference beyond this rule, the passage does not belong in Tier 2. Move it to the exclusion ledger.
Tier 3 — Conditional requirements
Include, and record the triggering condition verbatim or nearly so in its own field. This field is what makes a later NOT-APPLICABLE assessment auditable; a conditional requirement without its recorded trigger is unusable downstream.
Exclusion rule
Exclude:

* explanatory rationale;
* examples and worked illustrations, unless they instantiate a separately identified requirement;
* repeated restatements of a requirement already extracted within Volume 1;
* descriptive observations;
* aspirational language;
* implementation approaches the volume explicitly leaves open;
* stated non-claims;
* definitions that impose no operational or evidentiary consequence by themselves.

A definition that carries an operative consequence — a defined term whose definition itself constrains what may be claimed or done — is Tier 1 or Tier 2, not an excluded definition. Judge by consequence, not by grammatical form.
Within-volume duplication
Where several passages in Volume 1 express the same requirement, create one requirement row with multiple controlling citations. Do not create duplicate rows.
This is within-volume consolidation only. Cross-volume canonicalization happens in B1.4 and is not in scope here.
Artifact 1 — Volume 1 Requirement Register
One row per requirement. Fields:
ID — `V1-R001`, `V1-R002`, … sequential, stable, no gaps, assigned in order of first appearance in the manuscript.
Requirement statement — what the architecture requires, stated in the volume's own terms. Do not paraphrase into a stronger or more general obligation than the text supports.
Controlling text — exact quotation. Where multiple passages express the requirement, quote each.
Location(s) — section number and heading for each citation.
Tier — 1, 2, or 3.
Operative consequence if violated — required for Tier 2; optional but useful for Tier 1.
Triggering condition — required for Tier 3, verbatim or near-verbatim. Blank for Tiers 1 and 2.
Actor — who or what the requirement binds, where the text states or clearly implies it (the organization, the declaring function, an evaluator, an authorizing authority, a record, a system component). Where the text does not establish an actor, record `not stated`. Do not supply one.
Notes — only where something about the extraction needs recording: an ambiguity, a passage that nearly failed the inclusion test, a requirement whose scope is unclear from the text.
Artifact 2 — Volume 1 Requirement Exclusion Ledger
Not every sentence — only passages you considered requirement-like and deliberately excluded.
Fields:
Passage — short quotation or precise identification.
Location — section and heading.
Excluded because — one of: duplicate of [V1-Rnnn] / definition-only / rationale / example / descriptive observation / aspirational / implementation-open / stated non-claim / Tier 2 test failed (operative consequence could not be stated) / other (specify).
Note — one line where the exclusion was a close call.
The ledger's purpose is to make completeness reviewable. A later reader asking "why isn't §X in the register?" should find the answer here rather than needing to re-run extraction.
Unresolved extraction questions
Where a passage cannot be confidently included or excluded under ER-1.0, do not guess in either direction. Record it in a third short list:
Passage / Location / The question / Why the rule does not settle it
These are for the owner to adjudicate. An unresolved question is a better outcome than a silent inclusion or a silent exclusion.
Completion footer
End the pass with exactly this footer, completed:
Extraction rule version: ER-1.0 (2026-08-22) Extraction scope: entire Volume 1 Final Controlled Manuscript, beginning through end. Last section examined: [exact heading] Requirements extracted: N Candidate passages excluded: N Unresolved extraction questions: N Status: COMPLETE / NOT COMPLETE
`COMPLETE` means every section of the manuscript was examined under ER-1.0.
If the pass cannot finish — length, context, or any other reason — report `NOT COMPLETE`, state the exact section reached, and stop. A partial register reported as complete is worse than a partial register reported as partial: the next pass resumes from the identified location, and nothing downstream treats an incomplete denominator as whole.
Do not compress, sample, or summarize the manuscript to fit the pass into one response. If it does not fit, it does not fit, and the footer says so.
Output
Return the two artifacts, the unresolved-questions list, and the footer. Write them to a durable location in the repository under a Track B path, or return them for the owner to place — state which you did.
Nothing else. No summary of what the architecture requires, no observation about how much of it seems implemented, no recommendation.
