## Abstract

AI-mediated organizations act through people, professional roles, delegated authority, policies, information, sensors, controllers, software, legacy systems, workflows, credentials, physical processes, external dependencies, evidence, and accountable change. Models and agents are components inside that larger operating environment. The first engineering problem is therefore definitional: before an organization can evaluate operation or connect assurance to the enterprise that actually exists, it must define exactly what organizational reality is being governed.

Volume 1 establishes that governed target. The claim is the organizing object. It determines the claim-relevant system boundary, explicit assumptions, authorized purpose, authority, consequence classification, measures and measurands, intended external effects, and the Declared Organizational Operating Model against which later operation can be evaluated. AI Orchestration Governance: The Agent Is Not the System remains the public Volume 1 foundation and conceptual entry point. This manuscript is the controlled engineering realization of the Volume 1 allocation under Project Plan v0.2 — Frozen Architectural Baseline.

## Part I — Establish the Volume 1 Engineering Object

### 1. Inherited Premise and Engineering Problem

The AI agent is not the organizational system. A model or agent may perform its assigned task correctly while the organization fails because the wrong information was used, authority was invalid, a required control was bypassed, the action targeted the wrong object, the external effect did not occur, or the evidence cannot independently establish what happened. Local model correctness therefore does not establish organizational correctness.

Volume 1 asks: What exactly is the governed organizational reality against which operation, evidence, authority, capability, and organizational learning will later be evaluated? Definition precedes evaluation. The organization cannot determine whether evidence is adequate until it states the proposition the evidence must support; cannot define the item until it states the claim; and cannot treat task completion as success until it defines the intended external effect.

The claim determines the boundary. A model-only claim may justify a model-only boundary. A consequential organizational claim concerning safe, lawful, authorized, timely, or effective action generally requires a broader system containing every material component whose credible failure, omission, unmodeled behavior, or authority could change the truth, scope, or defensibility of the claim.

## Part III — Govern the Claim

### 4. Claim Formulation, Review, Authorization, and Contestability

Claim formulation is consequential because the claim determines the boundary, evidence burden, accepted assumptions, detectable failures, and what the organization may later say about itself. Consequential claims must be explicit, attributable, versioned, contestable, reviewed, authorized, and time-bound where applicable.

Proposal standing must be defined. Review must test clarity, scope, logical structure, authority, consequence, boundary adequacy, assumptions, measurand, intended effect, and downstream evaluability. Approval must be exercised by accountable authority. Contestability must be available to relevant professional, legal, engineering, operational, assurance, or oversight functions. A beneficiary of a narrow claim may not unilaterally exclude material failure paths.

| **Claim-governance function** | **Minimum obligation**                                                                                   |
|-------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Propose                       | State exact proposition, purpose, scope, rationale, intended effect, and proposed accountable authority. |
| Review                        | Challenge ambiguity, boundary, assumptions, consequence, authority, composition, and evaluability.       |
| Approve                       | Exercise accountable authority over the definition and effective version.                                |
| Contest                       | Permit competent functions to challenge scope, assumptions, authority, or composition.                   |
| Modify / suspend              | Create attributable version or status change without rewriting history.                                  |
| Supersede / retire            | End prospective applicability while preserving historical identity.                                      |

### 5. Organizational Claim Record and Lifecycle

The Organizational Claim Record is the anchor record for Volume 1. It preserves stable identity; exact proposition; purpose; accountable authority and steward; parent and constituent relationships; composition rule; scope; claim-relative item and boundary; consequence classification; assumptions; intended effects; measures and measurands; claim-level evidence requirements; acceptable degraded and prohibited conditions; authoritative sources; effective period; version; lifecycle status; modification, suspension, supersession, and retirement history; and downstream references.

| **Claim Record field group** | **Minimum information**                                                   |
|------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Identity / proposition       | Stable identity; exact proposition; title.                                |
| Governance                   | Purpose; accountable authority; steward; review; contestability.          |
| Structure / scope            | Parent/constituents; composition; population/operation/period/conditions. |
| Boundary / assumptions       | Item; exclusions; assumption references.                                  |
| Consequence                  | Classification and version.                                               |
| Measures / effects           | Measures; measurands; intended effects; timing/tolerance.                 |
| Evidence obligations         | Claim-level propositions and conditions ultimately to be demonstrable.    |
| Non-normal conditions        | Degraded conditions; fallbacks; prohibited conditions.                    |
| Sources / history            | Authoritative sources; effective period; version; lifecycle and history.  |
| Downstream references        | Applicable Volume 2 and Volume 3 references.                              |

| **Lifecycle state** | **Meaning**                                                   |
|---------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Proposed            | Standing for review, not authorized for reliance.             |
| Reviewed            | Required review completed; approval remains separate.         |
| Approved            | Accountable authority approved the defined version.           |
| Active              | Approved claim is within its effective conditions and period. |
| Modified            | Material change creates an attributable successor version.    |
| Suspended           | Prospective reliance is temporarily halted.                   |
| Superseded          | A later version replaces prospective applicability.           |
| Retired             | No longer governs new operation; history remains intact.      |

Material changes create attributable versions. A narrower successor does not erase a broader predecessor. Suspension, supersession, and retirement do not rewrite historical identity. The record is a functional information obligation, not a mandatory database.

| **Material-change / version trigger**                                 | **Required treatment**                                |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| Proposition, structure, or composition changes                        | Create attributable successor version.                |
| Scope, population, period, or boundary changes                        | Version claim and affected item/assumptions.          |
| Purpose, authority, consequence, measure/measurand, or effect changes | Version affected governed object and references.      |
| Authoritative source or declaration changes                           | Reassess lineage and version declaration as required. |
| Suspension, supersession, retirement                                  | Preserve historical identity and effective period.    |

### 6. Parent, Constituent, Compound, Conditional, and Population Claims

Claim logic must be declared before the outcome is known or, for retrospective work, reconstructed from authoritative historical artifacts. Volume 1 registers the logical relationship; Volume 2 determines evidentiary states under that relationship.

| **Claim form**            | **Required declaration**                                                                 |
|---------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Conjunctive               | Every required constituent.                                                              |
| Alternative / disjunctive | Valid alternatives and applicability conditions.                                         |
| Conditional               | Antecedent, consequent, and applicability conditions.                                    |
| Recurring / population    | Eligible population, period, denominator or inclusion rule, and aggregation proposition. |
| Informative proposition   | Explicitly non-required.                                                                 |

Claim-state composition and cross-episode aggregation remain Volume 2 functions. The evaluator may not invent or alter the registered composition rule after seeing the evidence.

## Part IV — Define the Claim-Relevant System

### 7. Claim-Relative Boundary and Item Definition

A component belongs inside the claim-relevant system whenever its credible failure, omission, unmodeled behavior, or authority could change the truth, scope, or defensibility of the claim. The boundary follows the claim, not the model, product, department, legal entity, or whole enterprise by default.

Included components may include models and agents; people and professional roles; claim authors, authority grantors, and reviewers; controllers; information sources and sensors; schemas and transformations; identity and time sources; software and legacy systems; workflows, credentials, routing, and gates; vendors, contractors, and external organizations; physical equipment and environmental conditions; execution and effect-observation paths; evidence stores; evaluators; measures; and oversight.

| **Boundary test**                                           | **Treatment**                                            |
|-------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Credible failure can change claim truth                     | Include or state an explicit defensible assumption.      |
| Authority can change legitimacy                             | Include the authority-bearing function.                  |
| Failure can change scope or defensibility                   | Include or explicitly bound the dependency.              |
| Technically connected but immaterial                        | Do not include solely because it is reachable.           |
| Model-only proposition                                      | A model-only boundary may be sufficient.                 |
| Organizational safe/lawful/authorized/effective proposition | Use the broader claim-relevant socio-technical boundary. |

### 8. Exclusions, Assumptions, and Runtime Dependence

A material component left outside the boundary does not disappear. It becomes an explicit assumption. Silence is undeclared reliance.

| **Assumption Register field** | **Obligation**                                    |
|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| Proposition                   | Exact condition relied upon.                      |
| Exclusion rationale           | Why the dependency remains outside the item.      |
| Dependent claim               | Claim or constituent relying on it.               |
| Basis / owner                 | Authoritative basis and responsible owner.        |
| Supporting evidence           | Evidence justifying reliance at declaration time. |
| Consequence if false          | Affected claim, authority, or condition.          |
| Class                         | Design-discharged or runtime-maintained.          |
| Period / conditions           | Effective period and applicability.               |
| Challenge method              | How it may be tested or contested.                |
| Trigger                       | Review or reclassification condition.             |

Design-discharged assumptions are sufficiently fixed by engineering evidence for the relevant claim and period. Runtime-maintained assumptions can change during operation and remain load-bearing. Volume 1 identifies and classifies assumptions. Volume 2 owns runtime reconciliation and the evidentiary consequence of failure. Volume 3 determines whether practical observation or intervention paths can connect the assurance architecture to those conditions.

### 9. Human, Professional, Controller, Workforce, and Privacy Boundary

Where a claim depends on professional judgment or human authorization, the declared role must identify competence or qualification, authority held, required information, timing, practical ability to reject, modify, redirect, delay, cancel, or escalate, conflicts or independence limits, and the effect the role is expected to produce. An Approve button is not a sufficient human-control model.

Controllers belong inside the claim-relevant system whenever their routing, context selection, credential release, policy application, boundary maintenance, or other behavior can change the claim-relevant outcome. Their inclusion does not move Runtime Control design or evaluation into Volume 1; it ensures the declared system does not treat a claim-relevant controller as an invisible referee.

Including people does not authorize generalized personnel surveillance. Identity is first-class evidence where needed to establish authorship, authority, decision, override, or material action. Otherwise the least identifying representation adequate for the legitimate assurance purpose must be used. Personnel adjudication remains with authorized management, professional, human-resources, labor, legal, compliance, investigative, or equivalent processes.

| **Role obligation**     | **Declared requirement**                                         |
|-------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Competence              | Qualification expected for the decision or observation.          |
| Authority               | Current authority needed for the role.                           |
| Information             | Minimum material context that must be available.                 |
| Timing                  | Time available before the relevant consequence.                  |
| Practical control       | Ability to reject, modify, redirect, delay, cancel, or escalate. |
| Conflict / independence | Known limitations affecting the role.                            |
| Expected effect         | What the role is expected to contribute to the claim.            |

## Part V — Purpose, Authority, Consequence, Measures, and Effects

### 10. Authorized Purpose

Authorized purpose is a governed object, not a mission slogan. It identifies source, accountable authority, permitted use, prohibited or out-of-scope use, intended beneficiary or mission result, relationship to the claim, information-access implications, authority implications, effective period, and change history.

| **Authorized Purpose Record field**   | **Required content**                    |
|---------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Source                                | Authoritative source.                   |
| Accountable authority                 | Authority approving bounded purpose.    |
| Permitted use                         | Allowed organizational use.             |
| Prohibited / out-of-scope use         | Uses not authorized.                    |
| Intended beneficiary / mission result | Who or what the use serves.             |
| Relationship to claim                 | How purpose constrains the proposition. |
| Information-access implications       | What information may be used/disclosed. |
| Authority implications                | Applicable or required authority.       |
| Effective period                      | When purpose applies.                   |
| Change history                        | Attributable changes.                   |

Purpose constrains what information may be used, what action may be taken, which authority applies, which people or systems may participate, what effect is legitimate, and what evidence may be retained or disclosed. Technical capability, repeated use, and model inference do not create purpose.

Neutral example. An organization may have a mission objective to maintain reliable service. An authorized purpose may permit a maintenance function to diagnose and correct a defined equipment condition for that objective. Authority to isolate or restart a particular asset must still come from the applicable authority source and holder; purpose does not grant it. A software agent may be technically capable of issuing a restart command, but technical capability creates neither the authorized purpose nor the authority to exercise that action.

### 11. Authority Definition, Delegation, and Seat-Layer View

Authority must be precise enough for downstream binding. The Authority Definition and Delegation Record identifies source, purpose, holder or actor, target, permitted and prohibited action, scope, limits, duration, delegation and subdelegation, conditions of use, required approval or evidence, revocation, expiration, exhaustion, supersession, and consequence.

Authority to act and evidence that action occurred are separate propositions. Evaluation does not create authority. Technical qualification does not create authority. Repeated operation does not create authority. Management preference does not cure missing legal, professional, delegated, or organizational authority.

Seat-layer doctrine may be used as a supporting analytical view. Authorship, granting and binding, execution, reconciliation, measure holding, evaluation, oversight, and governed change are loci where authority changes state. Seats are not mandatory departments, job titles, software services, or a second authority taxonomy.

| **Authority dimension**   | **Question to preserve**                                |
|---------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| Source                    | What valid organizational source creates the authority? |
| Purpose                   | For what legitimate use does it apply?                  |
| Holder / actor            | Who or what may exercise it?                            |
| Target / action           | Against what target and for what action?                |
| Scope / limits            | What is permitted and prohibited?                       |
| Time                      | When does it become effective, expire, or exhaust?      |
| Delegation                | What delegation or subdelegation is permitted?          |
| Conditions                | What approvals, evidence, or prerequisites apply?       |
| Revocation / supersession | How can authority cease or be replaced?                 |
| Consequence               | What consequence bounds its exercise?                   |

### 12. Consequence-Classification Governance

Consequence classification is governed configuration, not descriptive metadata. Its record identifies classification criteria, classification authority, supporting evidence, applicable claim or population, version, effective period, review period, exceptions, escalation, change authority, challenge process, and supersession history.

| **Consequence Classification Record field** | **Required content**                             |
|---------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Criteria                                    | Governed basis.                                  |
| Classification authority                    | Responsible functional authority.                |
| Evidence basis                              | Information supporting assignment.               |
| Scope                                       | Applicable claim/operation/population/condition. |
| Version / effective period                  | Current version and applicability.               |
| Review period                               | Scheduled review.                                |
| Exceptions / escalation                     | Governed treatment.                              |
| Change authority / challenge                | Who may change or contest.                       |
| Supersession history                        | Prior states preserved.                          |

Classification influences downstream assurance intensity, acceptable evidence quality, consequence-window requirements, meaningful human-review requirements, degraded-operation authority, automated-execution limits, effect-verification requirements, independence requirements, and evaluation effort. Volume 1 establishes and governs classification but does not design those downstream mechanisms.

| **Downstream dimension** | **Influence**                                                                                                                      |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Volume 2                 | Assurance intensity; evidence quality; consequence-window, human-review, independence, effect-verification, and evaluation effort. |
| Volume 3                 | Qualification burden; automated-execution limits; degraded conditions; active-mode ceilings; requalification sensitivity.          |
| Volume 1                 | Classification review, challenge, versioning, and source traceability; no downstream mechanism design.                             |

A lower classification may not be selected merely to reduce burden. A material mismatch between assumed and actual consequence becomes a first-class review condition. Volume 2 evaluates adequacy against operational evidence; Volume 3 binds the current classification to qualification and active mode.

### 13. Measures, Measurands, and Intended External Effects

For each claim-relevant measure, Volume 1 defines the property being measured, why that property supports the claim, what counts as satisfaction, acceptable evidence, threshold or decision rule, population or denominator, effective period, limitations, and measure-holding authority. The measurand must be precise enough that later evidence can establish or challenge the proposition without redefining success after the fact.

| **Definition element**    | **Obligation**                                             |
|---------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| Property                  | What is measured.                                          |
| Claim rationale           | Why it bears on the claim.                                 |
| Satisfaction rule         | What counts as satisfaction.                               |
| Acceptable evidence       | Evidence type in definition; sufficiency remains Volume 2. |
| Threshold / decision rule | Prospective boundary where applicable.                     |
| Population / denominator  | Applicable population/exposure.                            |
| Effective period          | Version/time applicability.                                |
| Limitations               | Known interpretation limits.                               |
| Measure-holding authority | Authority governing meaning.                               |

A performance measure can itself become part of the claim-relevant system because measures create organizational pressure. A locally rational productivity measure may conflict with safety, legality, quality, or professional duty. The function benefiting from a measure should not automatically become the sole authority over what the measure means.

The intended external effect is declared separately from recommendation, approval, command, dispatch, execution, acknowledgment, workflow completion, or record closure. It identifies the affected person, object, environment, or organizational state; required timing; acceptable tolerance; persistence or duration; acceptable degraded result; and relationship to the claim.

| **Stage**                 | **What it establishes**                                      |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Recommendation / proposal | A proposed course of action.                                 |
| Authorization             | Permission under applicable authority.                       |
| Dispatch                  | Release of the action into an execution path.                |
| Execution                 | The action was performed.                                    |
| Acknowledgment            | A downstream system or actor reported receipt or completion. |
| Intended external effect  | The required real-world state.                               |
| Verified external effect  | Volume 2 conclusion based on appropriate effect evidence.    |

Volume 2 evaluates measure fitness and verifies effect. Volume 3 qualifies the effect-observation path. Volume 1 performs neither.

## Part VI — Declare the Organizational Operating Model

### 14. The Declared Organizational Operating Model

The Declared Organizational Operating Model is the organization’s governed, versioned representation of how the claim-relevant operation is supposed to occur. It is not actual reality and need not be one universal enterprise model. Multiple governed artifacts or views may compose it if the complete claim-relevant declaration remains reconstructable.

| **Declared-model content** | **Minimum obligation**                                                      |
|----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Claim and structure        | Approved claim, constituent structure, and applicable version.              |
| Purpose and consequence    | Authorized purpose and consequence classification.                          |
| Boundary                   | Claim-relative system, exclusions, and assumptions.                         |
| Roles                      | Professional responsibilities and human-review conditions.                  |
| Authority                  | Sources, holders, transitions, conditions, and limits.                      |
| Context                    | Information sources, admissibility, access, and restrictions.               |
| Operation                  | Decision points, controls, gates, action and execution sequence.            |
| Dependencies               | External organizations, systems, physical conditions, and assumptions.      |
| Measures and effects       | Measures, measurands, intended effects, and effect-observation obligations. |
| Evidence                   | Claim-level evidence requirements.                                          |
| Non-normal paths           | Exceptions, degraded conditions, fallbacks, and prohibited paths.           |
| History                    | Version, effective period, and change history.                              |

No universal ontology, database, notation, workflow engine, or enterprise modeling tool is required. The architecture requires reconstructable governed meaning, not a particular representation technology. Volume 3 discovers the as-built and as-operated enterprise. Volume 2 compares the declaration with as-operated evidence.

### 15. Authoritative-Source-to-Declaration Fidelity, Exceptions, and Change

The governing lineage is Authoritative Organizational Source → Interpreted Requirement → Declared Organizational Operating Model. Authoritative sources may include law, regulation, delegated authority, policy, engineering requirements, professional standards, operating procedures, approved configurations, contracts, formally adopted measures, and other controlling organizational artifacts.

| **Source–Declaration Fidelity Record field** | **Required content**                           |
|----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Source identity / version                    | Controlling source and version.                |
| Effective period                             | Period source applies.                         |
| Interpreter / authority                      | Who performs or approves interpretation.       |
| Interpretation method                        | How source becomes requirement.                |
| Assumptions                                  | Material translation assumptions.              |
| Resulting declaration                        | Affected declared-model element.               |
| Validation / review evidence                 | Review evidence; sufficiency remains Volume 2. |
| Known limitations                            | Uncertainty or representational limits.        |
| Change history                               | Attributable changes and supersession.         |

Translation is consequential. Volume 1 preserves source identity and version, effective period, responsible interpreter or authority, interpretation method, assumptions, resulting declaration, validation or review evidence, known limitations, and change history. The translating function may contribute evidence about fidelity but may not conclusively self-certify fidelity where the claim depends on that translation. Volume 2 independently evaluates fidelity.

Legitimate exceptions, degraded conditions, fallbacks, and prohibited paths must be declared and versioned. Degraded operation must not silently become normal operation. Evidence may motivate change but does not enact it. Claims, boundaries, assumptions, authority definitions, classifications, measures, measurands, effects, and declared models change only through attributable versioned governance. Volume 2 owns the governed evidence-to-change machinery; Volume 1 does not create a separate organizational-learning loop.

| **Path condition** | **Volume 1 declaration obligation**                                                     |
|--------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Exception          | Scope, authority, rationale, effective period, affected elements, review trigger.       |
| Degraded condition | Condition, bounded operation, limits, affected measures/effects, expiry/review trigger. |
| Fallback           | Separately authorized alternative and use conditions; qualification remains Volume 3.   |
| Prohibited path    | Explicitly disallowed operation or condition; repetition does not create legitimacy.    |

| **Change trigger**                      | **Required Volume 1 response**                                      |
|-----------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Claim proposition changes               | Create attributable claim version.                                  |
| Boundary changes materially             | Version the item definition and dependent assumptions.              |
| Authority definition changes            | Version authority record and effective period.                      |
| Consequence classification changes      | Version classification and downstream references.                   |
| Measure or measurand changes            | Version satisfaction definition; do not rewrite historical results. |
| Authoritative source changes            | Reassess interpretation and declaration lineage.                    |
| Exception or degraded condition changes | Version scope, authority, period, and affected claim references.    |

## Part VIII — Volume Interfaces and Explicit Non-Ownership

The cross-volume interface preserves the difference between defining what must be true, evaluating whether evidence supports it, and engineering the paths through which the real enterprise can provide evidence or intervention.

Volume 1 records claim-level evidence requirements: the propositions and conditions the organizational claim must ultimately be capable of demonstrating. Volume 1 does not determine whether particular evidence is sufficient. Volume 2 determines evidence quality, sufficiency, independence, directness, timing, and consequence, and authors the functional Volume 2 → Volume 3 evidence or intervention contract. Volume 3 determines whether a complete capability path can satisfy that contract under the stated conditions.

Accountable Authorization may restrict operation but cannot manufacture evidence. Technical qualification does not create authority. Repeated operation does not create legitimacy. No volume silently takes another volume’s function.

## Part IX — Worked Example, Limits, Coverage, and Closing Architecture

### 19. Worked Example — Hazardous Equipment Condition

Organizational purpose: prevent operation of Equipment E while a defined hazardous condition exists and restore safe service only through valid authority and verified correction.

Parent Claim V1.0: For Operation X, the organization identified Hazardous Condition Y, acted under valid authority within 15 minutes of the qualifying signal, executed the approved intervention against the correct Equipment E, and achieved Effect Z — removal or effective containment of Condition Y — within 30 minutes.

Claim V1.0 becomes effective upon accountable approval and activation of the identified procedure, authority, configuration, and measure versions and remains effective until the earliest applicable suspension, supersession, retirement, expiry, or scheduled review trigger.

| **Constituent** | **Required proposition**                                    |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| C1              | Condition Y was correctly identified.                       |
| C2              | The decision and intervention were under valid authority.   |
| C3              | Effective intervention occurred within 15 minutes.          |
| C4              | The approved intervention was executed against Equipment E. |
| C5              | Effect Z occurred.                                          |
| C6              | Effect Z occurred within 30 minutes.                        |

The parent claim is conjunctive. Cost, productivity, and convenience measures are informative unless separately registered as required claims. Volume 1 registers this composition; it does not determine any constituent or parent claim state.

The consequence classification is high because failure can expose personnel or equipment to a hazardous condition and delay can narrow the preventive window. The classification record names criteria, accountable authority, evidence basis, version, effective period, and review trigger. No universal numerical consequence scale is implied.

The claim-relative boundary includes the initiating sensor and represented condition; asset identity mapping; governing procedure and authority sources; authorized professional decision role; controller or routing function; information package; credential or work-release path; maintenance system; field execution organization; Equipment E; downstream acknowledgment; independent physical effect-observation path; evidence custody; and review functions needed to support the claim. A productivity scoreboard is included because it can create pressure against the safety hold.

Explicit exclusion. The internal design and continuing physical state of the keyed isolation mechanism are excluded from direct runtime evaluation for Claim V1.0 only to the extent that the non-bypass property is treated as a design-discharged assumption supported by the identified engineering evidence for the effective period. If that basis no longer holds, the exclusion and the claim-relative boundary must be reconsidered.

| **Assumption**                                                                                                                               | **Class**          | **Reason / consequence**                                                                                                   |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| A physically keyed isolation mechanism cannot be bypassed without a separately observable physical intervention during the effective period. | Design-discharged  | Supported by engineering evidence for the defined period; if false, boundary and control assumptions must be reconsidered. |
| Initiating sensor remains sufficiently fresh for the claim.                                                                                  | Runtime-maintained | Staleness can invalidate identification or timing.                                                                         |
| Professional authority remains current.                                                                                                      | Runtime-maintained | Expired or revoked authority defeats C2.                                                                                   |
| Intervention path remains available inside the 15-minute condition.                                                                          | Runtime-maintained | Loss affects C3 and may constrain operation.                                                                               |
| Asset binding remains correct.                                                                                                               | Runtime-maintained | Wrong asset defeats C4 and potentially the parent claim.                                                                   |
| Required independent effect-observation path remains available.                                                                              | Runtime-maintained | Loss prevents the declared effect obligation from being demonstrated as designed.                                          |

Authority is defined by source, holder, scope, target, permitted intervention, prohibited actions, delegation, conditions, effective period, revocation or expiration, and required evidence. The professional role must receive the hazard identity, asset identity, current condition, governing restriction, relevant uncertainty, authority state, and time remaining, and must have practical ability to reject, redirect, delay, cancel, or escalate.

The primary measurand for the parent claim is not work-order closure. It is satisfaction of all required constituents, including effective intervention and timely Effect Z. Supporting measures include signal-to-effective-intervention time and intervention-to-effect time, with denominators and timing rules defined prospectively. A productivity measure may be tracked, but it cannot silently redefine the safety claim.

| **Worked-example element** | **Controlled definition**                                                                            |
|----------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Parent measurand           | Satisfaction of all six required constituents, including effective intervention and timely Effect Z. |
| Supporting measure         | Signal-to-effective-intervention time; prospective timing and denominator rules.                     |
| Supporting measure         | Intervention-to-effect time; prospective timing and denominator rules.                               |
| Execution evidence         | Approved intervention performed against Equipment E; not proof of Effect Z.                          |
| Intended effect            | Condition Y removed or effectively contained within 30 minutes and declared safe tolerance.          |
| Effect evidence obligation | Independent physical effect observation; sufficiency determined by Volume 2.                         |

The intended external effect is that Condition Y is removed or effectively contained on Equipment E within 30 minutes and remains within the declared safe tolerance for the specified observation period. A completion receipt is not proof of that effect.

The Declared Organizational Operating Model states the authorized sequence: qualifying signal → correct asset binding → context package → authorized professional decision → bounded authority release → intervention dispatch → execution against Equipment E → downstream acknowledgment → independent physical effect observation → evidence delivery to assurance. It also declares exception, degraded, fallback, and prohibited paths.

Authoritative-source lineage links each requirement to the governing procedure, delegated authority, engineering requirement, approved configuration, and adopted measure in force for V1.0. Translation and declaration versions remain reconstructable.

Acceptable degraded condition: if the automated maintenance interface is unavailable but a separately authorized and qualified human fallback remains available within the same intervention window and remains reconstructable, operation may use that fallback. Volume 1 declares that such a fallback may be legitimate; Volume 3 determines whether the actual fallback path is qualified. Loss of timely intervention capability, current authority, correct asset binding, or the required effect-observation obligation is not silently treated as equivalent normal operation.

Volume 1 handoff: Volumes 2 and 3 receive Claim V1.0 and constituent structure; authorized purpose; high consequence classification; claim-relative boundary; assumption register; authority definitions; professional-role requirements; measure and measurand definitions; intended Effect Z; Declared Organizational Operating Model; authoritative-source lineage; claim-level evidence obligations; acceptable degraded and prohibited conditions; effective versions; and lifecycle status. Volume 1 stops here. It does not assign a claim state, determine whether the operation actually occurred as declared, determine evidence sufficiency, or design the technical capability path.

| **Governed-target handoff element** | **Delivered by Volume 1**                                          |
|-------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Claim                               | V1.0, six constituents, conjunctive structure, effective period.   |
| Purpose / consequence               | Authorized purpose and high consequence classification.            |
| Boundary / assumptions              | Boundary, explicit exclusion, Assumption Register.                 |
| Authority / role                    | Authority definitions and professional-role requirements.          |
| Measures / effects                  | Measures/measurands; intended Effect Z and timing.                 |
| Declared model / lineage            | Declared Organizational Operating Model and source lineage.        |
| Evidence obligations                | Claim-level propositions/conditions; no sufficiency determination. |
| Non-normal operation                | Degraded/fallback and prohibited conditions.                       |
| Versions / lifecycle                | Applicable versions, effective period, lifecycle status.           |

### 20. Engineering Limits and Residual Conditions

Volume 1 cannot guarantee that authoritative sources are complete, that every material dependency has been discovered, that declared authority will remain valid, that the enterprise can produce the evidence later required, or that the Declared Organizational Operating Model matches as-operated reality. Those are visible assumptions, downstream assurance questions, Volume 3 integration questions, or implementation constraints — not permission to manufacture certainty.

| **Residual condition**                                                                                                                                           | **Classification / owner**                                                      |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Evidence-quality, sufficiency, independence, directness, timing, claim-state composition, effect verification, evaluator governance, or governed learning issue  | Volume 2 Engineering Issue.                                                     |
| Evidence acquisition, interface, legacy-system, discovery, scalability, privacy, as-operated connectivity, path qualification, or continuing-qualification issue | Volume 3 Integration Issue.                                                     |
| Cost, staffing, local role assignment, tooling, data models, latency, resources, organizational resistance, deployment feasibility                               | Implementation Constraint unless evidence shows architecture is invalid.        |
| Material contradiction of the controlling architecture or unsupported load-bearing assumption                                                                    | Architecture Defect; only this class can justify reopening the frozen baseline. |

Implementation difficulty alone does not reopen the architecture. Volume 1 must preserve unresolved limitations rather than silently repairing them through new architecture. The correct response is to classify the issue, keep it visible, and allow the responsible downstream volume or implementation process to address it.

The three volumes are analytically separable and operationally interdependent. Volume 1 defines the governed organizational target. Volume 2 independently evaluates operation, evidence, claim states, baselines, effects, findings, and whether the operating model remains appropriate. Volume 3 connects those requirements to the heterogeneous as-built and as-operated enterprise and maintains the qualification of complete capability paths.

No single operational component becomes final authority over evidence used to establish its own correctness. Volume 1 does not manufacture assurance by definition. Volume 2 does not assume evidence the enterprise cannot provide. Volume 3 does not weaken an assurance requirement because integration is inconvenient. The architecture remains evolutionary, but governed change does not erase historical claims, versions, assumptions, or limitations.

Define reality. Evaluate reality. Connect reality.
