## Abstract

AI-mediated organizations do not operate against architecture diagrams. They operate against an enterprise that has accumulated over years or decades: modern services beside legacy databases, vendor systems beside local spreadsheets, automated workflows beside professional judgment, networked sensors beside physical inspection, documented procedures beside workarounds, and formal authority beside the actual paths through which work is done. Volume 3 addresses the engineering problem created by that reality. It defines the bounded execution and enterprise-interaction layer through which claim-relevant capabilities discover, qualify, reach, observe, coordinate with, and act upon the as-built and as-operated enterprise.

The engineering object is the complete claim-relevant capability path, not the agent, model, connector, source, adapter, or interface in isolation. A path includes whatever is materially necessary for the requirement to become organizational evidence or organizational action: identity and binding, context, authority, credentials, transformations, routing, execution, effect observation, custody, recovery, and the dependencies capable of defeating the result. Qualification is therefore requirement-relative, configuration-bound, scope-bound, condition-bound, and time-bound. The same interface may be sufficient for one requirement and insufficient for another; timely for reconstruction but too late for prevention; available but only indirectly; or technically functional while not independently defensible.

Volume 3 is organized around a continuing lifecycle: Discover; Design; Qualify; Authorize; Activate; Observe; Evaluate Capability Health; Maintain, Restrict, or Degrade; Requalify After Material Change; and Retire, Replace, or Restore. Within DRNT, this bounded execution and enterprise-interaction layer is Layer L3. DRNT supplies the originating architectural context and principal engineering inheritance, but Volume 3 remains technology-neutral. It does not require one agent framework, one integration platform, one data architecture, one global identity, one ontology, one data lake, universal connectivity, or cloud migration.

Volume 3 makes L3 continuously qualifiable and auditable. Volume 2 independently determines what the resulting evidence permits the organization to claim. The architecture therefore refuses two shortcuts: technical availability does not become authority, and missing or weak evidence does not become manufactured assurance. Its purpose is to make the real enterprise governable on the terms on which it actually exists.

## Part I — Establish the L3 Engineering Object

### 1. Inherited Inputs and Volume Boundary

Volume 3 begins after the organization has already answered two prior questions. Volume 1 has defined the governed target: the approved organizational claim, its purpose, claim-relative system boundary, governing authority, material assumptions, intended external effects, consequence classification, and declared operating model. Volume 2 has defined the assurance burden: what must be knowable, when it must be knowable, what quality and independence the evidence must possess, whether intervention must remain possible inside a consequence window, and what evidentiary consequences follow if a required condition cannot be established.

Those inputs are not background context. They determine the engineering problem Volume 3 is permitted to solve. If Volume 2 requires independently defensible evidence that a physical effect occurred, Volume 3 must find or construct a path capable of producing that evidence. If Volume 2 requires intervention before an irreversible consequence, Volume 3 must determine whether a technically and organizationally valid path can act inside that window. If no such path exists, the correct output is a limitation, not a weaker substitute silently treated as equivalent.

Throughout the L3 lifecycle, the complete capability path remains bound to the governing objects that give its operation meaning: the approved organizational claim; authorized purpose; applicable authority; consequence classification; declared operating model; required external effects; applicable measures and measurands; evidence requirements; effective versions; and current baseline status. Volume 3 does not redefine those objects. It preserves their binding to the path so that capability, drift, and limitation remain visible relative to the organization’s governed target.

The division of responsibility is therefore load-bearing. Volume 3 owns L3 engineering qualification and capability health. Volume 2 owns independent organizational assurance. Volume 3 determines whether a source, interface, worker, or complete path can perform a defined function under stated conditions and whether the properties supporting that qualification remain healthy. Volume 2 determines whether the qualification is itself defensible, whether the organization operated as authorized and intended, whether the required effect occurred, what the evidence permits the organization to claim, and whether the declared operating model remains appropriate.

Accountable Authorization crosses the boundary without collapsing it. Technical qualification establishes a ceiling: what the path has been demonstrated to support. Accountable authority may permit less than that ceiling because of policy, consequence, residual risk, or organizational choice. It may not permit more than qualification supports, and it may not strengthen evidence. Qualification is not authorization. Authorization is not evidence.

### 2. Why the Execution Layer Was Deferred

The execution layer was deliberately deferred because capability is hazardous to design before purpose, authority, evidence, and consequence are known. A system cannot responsibly decide how to reach enterprise systems, release credentials, use a UI agent, route professional judgment, collect evidence, or verify effects until the architecture knows what claim is being made and what must remain true for that claim to be supportable.

The required sequence is simple: define the governed reality; define the assurance burden; then design bounded execution and enterprise interaction. This is containment-before-capability. It prevents technical convenience from determining the governance architecture. An API is not preferred merely because it is clean. A UI agent is not acceptable merely because it can click the same screen as a person. A human report is not inferior merely because it is not machine-generated. A modern data lake is not automatically superior to a source-resident record. The requirement governs the path.

Deferral also prevents a common category error: treating L3 as a roaming-agent layer. An agent can be one worker inside a capability path, but the path also contains the conditions that make the worker's output usable: authority, target identity, context, transformations, credentials, timing, downstream acknowledgments, effect observation, evidence custody, and failure behavior. Designing the agent first and governing the rest later reverses the architecture.

### 3. The Complete Claim-Relevant Capability Path

The complete claim-relevant capability path is the unit of engineering and continuing qualification. It is the path through which a Volume 3 capability becomes organizational evidence or organizational action. Depending on the requirement, it may include the worker or agent; model and version; deterministic logic; workflow contract; capability-registry entry; source and target bindings; context sources and packages; transformations; authority state; credential-release path; routing and gating decisions; API, database, event, UI/computer-use, human, professional, physical, offline, external, or hybrid interfaces; retries, recovery, and override behavior; dispatch; execution; acknowledgment; downstream state; effect observation; evidence custody; independent sentinels; and material common dependencies.

This broader object is necessary because component correctness does not compose automatically into path correctness. A model can produce a correct recommendation while the target binding is wrong. A source can be responsive while stale. A transformation can preserve syntax while changing meaning. A UI agent can report that it clicked Submit while the downstream transaction never committed. A professional can receive complete information after the relevant consequence window has closed. A work system can record completion while the physical condition remains unchanged.

The engineering invariant is therefore: the unit of continuing qualification is the complete claim-relevant capability path through which L3 behavior becomes organizational action or organizational evidence. The implication is practical. Qualification evidence must be capable of showing not merely that each component worked in isolation, but that the assembled path retained the properties the requirement depends upon.

### 4. The Volume 2 → Volume 3 Evidence and Intervention Contract

Volume 2 specifies the requirement functionally. For evidence, it identifies the proposition that must be knowable, the claim or authority that depends upon it, the required provenance, integrity, freshness, completeness, attribution, directness, independence, and timing, and the consequence if the requirement cannot be met. For intervention, it additionally identifies what must be constrained, the time by which intervention must become effective, the authority required to act, and the evidence needed to show that the intervention actually took effect.

Volume 3 returns a requirement-relative engineering result. The result belongs to the tuple: Requirement + End-to-End Path or Path Set + Configuration + Scope + Operating Conditions + Effective Period. This prevents capability from becoming an intrinsic label attached to an interface or source. The same database view may be Available as required for retrospective reconstruction and Available too late for preventive control. The same vendor feed may be usable for trend analysis but Not independently defensible for a claim about its own completeness.

The six controlling capability results are:

| **Capability result**                     | **Engineering interpretation**                                                                                                                                                                    |
|-------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Available as required                     | The qualified path satisfies the defined requirement within the stated configuration, scope, operating conditions, and effective period.                                                          |
| Available with limitations                | The path is usable for the defined requirement but carries material limitations that remain explicit and do not fit a more specific controlling result.                                           |
| Available only through indirect evidence  | The requirement can be approached only through an inferential chain rather than the direct evidence path otherwise required.                                                                      |
| Available too late for preventive control | The path can support reconstruction or post-consequence assurance but cannot reliably support effective intervention inside the required consequence window.                                      |
| Not independently defensible              | The path may produce information, but the evidence needed for the proposition cannot be defended independently of a claim-relevant component whose correctness it is intended to establish.       |
| Unavailable                               | No presently qualified path has been identified within the recorded claim-relative discovery scope, authority envelope, access conditions, operating period, and known enterprise representation. |

Available with limitations is not a masking state. It may not conceal that evidence is indirect, too late for preventive control, or not independently defensible. Where several paths contribute, the qualification preserves path-specific results, conjunctive and alternative dependencies, material orthogonal limitations, and the rule used to determine the controlling result.

Capability result, organizational claim state, and Graduated Integration mode remain separate. A path can be Available as required while the organizational claim is still Unsupported because some different required condition failed. A path can be technically qualified for a higher mode while accountable authority restricts it to a lower mode. The architecture depends on keeping those questions distinct.

## Part II — Discover the Enterprise That Actually Exists

### 5. Claim-Relative Discovery

Discovery begins from the exact evidence or intervention requirement, not from a catalog of available technology. The first question is not which systems can be connected. It is what proposition must be established or what intervention must become possible for the governing claim. Only then does Volume 3 search the enterprise for candidate paths.

Candidate paths may exist in documented systems, historical records, current configuration, application logs, transaction histories, identity systems, work orders, maintenance records, professional practice, local spreadsheets, shared drives, sensors, physical inspection, contractor systems, external organizations, offline equipment, or institutional knowledge. A path that exists only because an experienced employee knows which office to call is still part of the as-operated enterprise if the organization relies on it.

Discovery must preserve its own limitations. The record identifies the requirement, search boundary, methods used, candidate systems and people considered, access restrictions, authority constraints, legal and privacy limits, rejected paths and reasons, professional validation, residual unknowns, and continuing-discovery triggers. This record defines the scope within which Unavailable may be returned. The failure of the first preferred integration mechanism is not Unavailable.

Continuing discovery is permanent because the as-operated enterprise never permanently converges. Systems are replaced, interfaces drift, local tools appear, vendors change, workarounds emerge, records disappear, and new dependencies become material. A stable enterprise map is therefore a versioned hypothesis, not a permanent truth.

### 6. Historical Runtime Evidence and Baseline Bootstrap

Historical Runtime Evidence is often the most practical starting point for discovering the as-operated enterprise because organizations already possess years of traces that reveal how work actually moved. Application logs, transaction histories, database audit trails, identity records, work orders, incidents, approvals, inspections, maintenance records, alarms, workflow histories, and operational reports can expose relationships that current documentation does not contain.

Its principal use is to bootstrap and challenge the as-operated representation. It can also support retrospective evaluation, post-incident reconstruction, historical path qualification, latency analysis, drift detection, recurring-pattern analysis, and organizational learning. Those additional uses do not change its basic limitation: recorded history is not complete operational history.

Three provenance descriptors prevent historical reconstruction from overstating itself. A Recorded fact is a proposition present in a record; its trustworthiness still depends on provenance, integrity, attribution, time, and other evidence properties. A Derived relationship is inferential and retains its derivation, evidence basis, uncertainty, and limitations. An Unresolved condition is a condition the available record cannot resolve. None is a claim state, and none bypasses Volume 2 evidence-quality and sufficiency rules.

Historical recurrence also has no authority-generating effect. A workaround used for years may reveal how the organization actually operates, but repeated practice does not convert that workaround into authorized procedure. The distinction between descriptive reality and legitimate authority must survive the bootstrap process.

### 7. Designed, As-Built, and As-Operated

Volume 3 uses three enterprise realization views. Designed describes how the system was intended to exist. As-Built describes what was actually implemented and configured. As-Operated describes how the implemented system actually participates and functions over time. These are distinct from the Declared, Observed, and Assured baselines used by the governance and assurance architecture.

The two sets of concepts answer different questions. Designed, As-Built, and As-Operated ask what the enterprise was intended to be, what was implemented, and how it actually behaves. Declared, Observed, and Assured ask what the organization authoritatively says should happen, what evidence repeatedly shows does happen, and what Volume 2 independently determines is sufficiently defensible. The relationship matters, but interchangeability would create a governance defect.

The provisional as-operated representation produced during discovery is therefore bootstrap information, not a fourth authoritative baseline. It can reveal undocumented dependencies, local tools, informal handoffs, human workarounds, and configuration divergence. It may become increasingly accurate, but accuracy does not make it authority.

Reconciliation among the three realization views is valuable because it exposes where the enterprise has changed without its formal representation changing with it. Those differences are not automatically failures. They are evidence that the organization needs to understand which reality is intended, which is authorized, and which must be qualified.

## Part III — Design and Qualify the Capability Path

### 8. Path Design

Path design converts a requirement and candidate path set into a versioned end-to-end design. The design identifies the selected path or path set, alternatives, conjunctive and optional dependencies, operating envelope, source and target bindings, transformations, authority and credential chain, timing, retries, recovery, effect-observation path, evidence path, common dependencies, and instrumentation.

The design reuses inherited DRNT mechanisms where they already satisfy the function. Action identity, context packages, capability registration, source-event identity, credential gating, receipts, append-only audit, workflow contracts, WAL-related controls, and governed-change mechanisms should be extended rather than duplicated. Volume 3 adds L3-specific information only where heterogeneous enterprise interaction creates a real need.

L3 extensions do not create a second authority plane, claim-state system, independent organizational assurance evaluator, audit spine, organizational-learning loop, or governed-mutation process. Where heterogeneous enterprise interaction requires extension, the inherited mechanism remains the governing mechanism unless a demonstrated need requires a bounded extension.

Legacy and non-software mechanisms are first-class design choices. A read-only database view may be excellent for reconstruction but too stale for preventive use. A UI agent may be the only practical interface to an old application and therefore require stronger target binding, credential limitation, visual-state checks, and separate effect observation. A professional confirmation may be the strongest available evidence for a physical condition but depend on competence, contemporaneity, independence, and custody.

Path design also includes failure behavior. A design that specifies only the nominal path is incomplete. It must identify what happens when the source is unavailable, the transformation fails, the target rejects the action, the acknowledgment is ambiguous, the effect cannot be observed, the authority changes, or a common dependency becomes unreliable.

### 9. Cross-System Binding

Connectivity is not sufficient if the connected records refer to different entities, periods, meanings, or events. Cross-system binding therefore treats identity, time, semantic meaning, representation, episode membership, and transformation provenance as first-class engineering problems.

Identity binding may require mapping an asset, person, case, work order, or event across systems that use different identifiers or different levels of granularity. Time binding must distinguish condition-effective time, first observable time, acquisition time, receipt time, detection time, record-update time, and notification time where the distinction affects the requirement. Semantic binding must preserve how one representation was translated into another.

Original and transformed representations remain linked to the method and version that produced the transformation. This prevents a derived field, normalized code, model-generated summary, or mapped identifier from silently becoming the source fact. A transformation that cannot be reconstructed is a qualification limitation even when the output appears plausible.

Binding is claim-relative. A coarse identity mapping may be sufficient for a low-consequence trend analysis and inadequate for a consequential intervention against a specific asset or person. Qualification therefore tests the binding actually required by the claim rather than treating identity resolution as a universal background service.

### 10. Requirement–Path Qualification

Qualification asks whether the complete requirement-relative path can perform its defined function under the stated operating conditions. It tests function, timing, integrity, availability, binding, reconstruction, transformation validity, effect-observation capability, failure behavior, recovery, independence limitations, and material common dependencies.

The Requirement–Path Qualification Record preserves the qualification method, supporting evidence, assumptions, limitations, responsible engineering authority, test results, effective period, review status, challenge status, requalification triggers, and state history. A favorable result is therefore not a permanent attribute of an interface. It is an engineering conclusion bounded to a path, configuration, scope, condition, and time.

Where the path is composite, its logic must be explicit. Conjunctive dependencies require every required constituent to satisfy the relevant property. Alternative paths require a rule stating when substitution is valid. A fallback that is slower may preserve post-consequence evidence while losing preventive capability. A human fallback may preserve authority but reduce reconstruction quality. Those differences remain visible in the qualification.

Volume 3 owns the qualification but does not certify it conclusively. The qualification record must remain inspectable and challengeable by Volume 2 Independent Evaluation. A source, worker, agent, adapter, or platform may contribute evidence about its own performance; it may not become the sole final authority over capability where its failure could conceal the loss of a required property.

### 11. Common-Mode Dependencies and Falsification Paths

A path can appear healthy while a shared dependency corrupts operation and the evidence used to judge operation. Volume 3 therefore maintains a claim-relative view of common dependencies affecting identity, time, network, storage, transformations, credentials, external providers, human review, evidence acquisition, qualification, evaluation, and effect observation.

The governing question is: if this dependency fails, lies, drifts, omits, delays, or is controlled by the wrong authority, what path remains capable of revealing the error? The purpose is not universal duplication. Full redundancy everywhere would create cost and complexity without necessarily improving independence.

The requirement is proportionate falsification. A high-consequence path may justify an alternate sensor, independent external record, physical confirmation, separate time source, or segmented evidence channel. A lower-consequence reversible path may reasonably accept a shared dependency if the residual limitation is explicit and the consequence does not justify stronger separation.

Where no meaningful challenge path exists, that fact becomes a standing limitation of qualification. The architecture does not treat architectural neatness as evidence that a common-mode dependency is safe.

## Part IV — Authorize and Activate

### 12. Qualification Ceiling, Authorization Ceiling, and Active Mode

Three distinct states govern activation. The technical qualification ceiling states what engineering evidence demonstrates the path can perform. The accountable authorization ceiling states what the organization permits. The effective active mode is the most restrictive result of qualification, authorization, and current degradation status.

This distinction prevents two forms of authority laundering. Engineering cannot convert technical capability into organizational permission. Management cannot authorize a capability beyond what the evidence demonstrates. A path qualified for a higher mode may remain deliberately restricted. A previously authorized path may be demoted when source health, independence, timing, reconstruction, or effect observation degrades.

Activation binds the actual path identity, configuration, model and tool versions where relevant, authority state, credentials, sentinels, evidence paths, operating envelope, and the governing claim, authorized purpose, consequence classification, declared operating model, required effects, applicable measures and measurands, evidence requirements, effective versions, and current baseline status before use. Mismatch between the qualified object and the activated object produces a hold, restriction, or explicitly authorized alternate path.

The active state is therefore reconstructable. A later reviewer must be able to determine not only what the organization intended to activate, but what exact path, version, authority, and evidence regime was in force when the operation occurred.

### 13. Graduated Integration

Graduated Integration governs how a defined capability participates in organizational work. It is capability-scoped rather than organization-wide, and it does not assume that greater autonomy is the desired endpoint. The mode reflects the interaction of demonstrated technical capability, accountable authority, consequence, reversibility, and current health.

| **Mode**                                                             | **Engineering meaning**                                                                                                                                                                           |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Observe Only                                                     | Acquire, preserve, or route authorized evidence without producing analytical conclusions that drive action.                                                                                       |
| 2\. Analyze / Correlate                                              | Relate evidence, identify patterns, or construct bounded analytical products without becoming the decision authority.                                                                             |
| 3\. Recommend                                                        | Produce a bounded recommendation for an authorized human or organizational decision function.                                                                                                     |
| 4\. Prepare Action for Approval                                      | Prepare a specific action, transaction, command, or artifact, but hold consequential execution for accountable approval.                                                                          |
| 5\. Execute Bounded Reversible Actions                               | Execute within demonstrated authority where consequence is bounded, the action is effectively reversible, and the required evidence and recovery paths remain healthy.                            |
| 6\. Execute Higher-Consequence Actions Within Demonstrated Authority | Execute higher-consequence actions only within a specifically demonstrated and accountable authority envelope, with the required intervention, reconstruction, and effect-observation properties. |

The modes map to existing DRNT workflow contracts, capability controls, and WAL machinery where those mechanisms satisfy the function. Volume 3 does not create a second general trust engine. Movement between modes is evidence-based, governed, reversible, and tied to the defined capability and scope.

Material loss of independence, failed effect verification, interface drift, source-health degradation, timing failure, authority change, model, prompt, tool, schema, or policy change, loss of reconstruction capability, or unexplained common-mode behavior requires restriction or requalification as appropriate to the affected requirement. The active mode is not an entitlement earned permanently by past success.

## Part V — Operate and Observe the Actual Path

### 14. Runtime Integration Envelope

The Runtime Integration Envelope is the event-level binding between the qualified design and the operation that actually occurred. It exists because the organization must be able to reconstruct the real path, not merely point to the design that should have governed it.

For evidence acquisition, the envelope preserves as applicable the governing requirement, evaluation episode, evidence identity, source artifact or protected reference, source and represented condition, actual path used, interface and configuration versions, acquisition method, originating time, collection and receipt times, temporal uncertainty, cross-system binding, transformation history, original and transformed representations, integrity anchors, custody transitions, access and purpose restrictions, and known limitations.

For intervention, the envelope additionally preserves target and target binding, requested action, authority reference, credentials used, preconditions, the actual routing and gating decision, applicable rule and version, gate result, discrepancy-controlled redirect where applicable, override or exception that changed the qualified path, the authority for that override or exception, its scope and effective period, dispatch, acknowledgment, retries and errors, rollback or containment, evidence that the intervention became effective, linked effect-observation evidence, and any resulting active-mode or evidence consequences.

The Runtime Integration Envelope is a functional information obligation, not necessarily a new database. Existing DRNT action envelopes, context packages, source-event identities, receipt chains, capability records, and audit events should supply the facts where they already do so. Volume 3 must not create a competing account of the operation merely to satisfy a new document label.

### 15. Evidence Custody and Federated Reality

Volume 3 owns acquisition, transport, transformation, provenance preservation, identity and time binding, custody transition, and delivery into the assurance architecture. Volume 2 owns claim-relative evidence handling after receipt, preservation needed for evaluation and historical claim-state integrity, protected access, and use in Independent Evaluation.

This boundary permits evidence to remain source-resident, federated, replicated, physically preserved, externally held, escrowed, or transferred to a protected assurance store. A universal evidence repository is neither required nor necessarily desirable. In many domains, the strongest evidence remains under the custody of the authoritative system, professional office, external organization, or physical archive that created it.

Federated custody does not weaken the evidentiary burden. The record must preserve where the evidence resides, how it can be accessed, what transformations occurred, which party controls custody, what integrity anchor exists, and what legal, privacy, contractual, or technical limitations constrain access.

Evidence that exists but is inaccessible inside the required evaluation window can still be operationally unavailable. Custody therefore belongs inside capability qualification rather than being treated as an administrative afterthought.

### 16. Human, Professional, Physical, Offline, External, and Hybrid Paths

Many enterprise facts cannot be acquired from software alone, and many consequential interventions cannot be completed by software alone. Human, professional, physical, offline, external, and hybrid paths are therefore ordinary L3 mechanisms when the enterprise depends on them.

A human or professional evidence path may require identity, role, competence, relationship to the condition, observation method, contemporaneity, authority or custody, independence, incentive conditions, availability, contradiction handling, corroboration, privacy restrictions, and known limitations. A professional judgment recorded in a system is not automatically reducible to the database field that stores it; the claim may depend on the competence and circumstances under which the judgment was formed.

A human or hybrid intervention path may require evidence of correct recipient, successful delivery, comprehensibility, valid authority, acknowledgment, sufficient time, practical ability to reject, alter, stop, redirect, or escalate, action actually taken, and resulting effect. The functional term is effective intervention path because the relevant mechanism can be technical, human, or hybrid.

Offline and isolated systems also remain governable without forcing connectivity. They may participate through one-way evidence, read-only feeds, controlled transfer, physical measurement, professional reporting, or deliberately separated boundaries. Integration is not the universal objective. Governed evidence and bounded interaction are.

### 17. Governed Situational Awareness and Context Delivery

One important output of L3 integration is a claim-relative, evidence-qualified connected operating picture assembled for a legitimate organizational function. Whole-enterprise situational awareness does not mean that every person, model, agent, or central office sees everything. It means the architecture can assemble the claim-relevant picture needed by a legitimate function while preserving local authority, privacy, professional boundaries, purpose limitation, and need-to-know.

Context delivery is therefore filtered by role, authority, mission, consequence, need-to-know, and operating context. A field worker may need a location, hazard, and immediate action constraint. An engineer may need current condition, history, related signals, and technical limitations. A supervisor may need authority status, unresolved conflicts, timing, and options. An evaluator may need the complete claim-relevant evidence path. The same enterprise fact can legitimately produce different context packages for different functions.

Context packages carry more than content. They should preserve source, freshness, limitations, conflicts, unavailable required elements, transformation history, and applicable access or authority restrictions. Material information that was withheld or unavailable must remain visible in the record together with the policy or authority governing that condition.

Centralized awareness must not become centralized authority. A system may be capable of assembling a whole-of-organization picture without giving one actor the authority to decide across every domain. The architecture connects evidence across controlled boundaries while preserving the authority structure that gives the evidence meaning.

### 18. Workforce, Privacy, and the Observation Boundary

Baseline observation, continuing discovery, and capability-health observation are directed at claim-relevant organizational operations and system conditions, not generalized employee surveillance. Evidence minimization, purpose limitation, need-to-know, access control, privacy, applicable labor obligations, and legal limits are therefore Volume 3 integration requirements.

Individual identity is first-class evidence when identity is materially relevant to the claim: for example, when a person or office authored, granted, rejected, overrode, changed, or exercised claim-relevant authority. Outside those conditions, the architecture must use the least identifying representation adequate for the legitimate assurance purpose.

This distinction protects the object of assurance. A team may appear slow because it is reconciling conflicting policies it did not author. A reviewer may appear compliant because workload and evidence presentation made independent judgment structurally improbable. A safety office may appear obstructive because it is the only function capable of seeing a contradiction. The primary engineering question is what organizational system produced the observed operation.

Where operational evidence raises concerns about individual conduct, Volume 3 preserves the claim-relevant evidence and routes personnel judgment through the organization's authorized management, professional, human-resources, labor, legal, compliance, investigative, or equivalent process. L3 does not create a parallel personnel-adjudication system merely because operational evidence involves a person.

## Part VI — Evaluate L3 Capability Health

### 19. Continuing Qualification

Qualification at deployment establishes an initial state, not permanent fitness. Sources change, interfaces drift, models and tools are updated, authority changes, professional roles change, network and identity dependencies change, evidence paths weaken, and the environment can move outside the conditions under which the original tests were performed.

Continuing qualification monitors the properties on which the qualification depends and preserves evidence sufficient to determine when those properties ceased to hold. The monitored object is not merely component uptime. It includes timing, integrity, binding, reconstruction, transformation correctness, independence, effect observation, recovery, and material common dependencies.

Continuing qualification also tests whether the active path remains bound to the governing claim, authorized purpose, authority, consequence classification, declared operating model, required effects, measures and measurands, evidence requirements, effective versions, and current baseline status. A mismatch or drift in those bindings is capability-health evidence. Volume 3 makes the drift visible and applies the required engineering restriction or requalification; it does not independently redefine the mission, claim, measure, or baseline.

L3 capability-health evaluation asks whether the path remains inside its qualified operating envelope and whether maintenance, restriction, degradation, requalification, replacement, or retirement is required. It does not decide whether the organizational claim remains Supported, Narrowed, Indeterminate, Unsupported, or Contradicted / Failed. That remains Volume 2.

The distinction is important during degradation. Volume 3 can determine that a path lost preventive timing at 14:32. Volume 2 determines what that loss means for claims about operations conducted after 14:32, including whether the evidence supports only post-consequence assurance or a narrower proposition.

### 20. Silent Degradation

Obvious outage and silent failure require different engineering treatment. An unavailable source is visible. A stale but responsive source can continue producing plausible values. A missing event can leave a log internally consistent. An interface can change one label while the automation continues clicking the wrong control. A human review process can remain formally intact while practical review quality collapses.

Silent degradation includes stale-but-responsive sources, incomplete records, selective omission, semantic transformation failure, misattribution, silent interface change, clock drift, degraded independence, missing sentinels, weakening human review, adapter behavior change, model or tool change, policy or authority change, and effect-observation failure.

A material degradation mode that cannot be detected or independently challenged is itself a standing limitation of the path. The architecture cannot claim continuing qualification for a property that it has no meaningful way to observe or falsify.

Temporal reconstruction is also essential. Condition-effective time, first observable time, detection time, record-update time, notification time, and temporal uncertainty remain distinct. A later restoration does not rewrite the historical degraded interval.

### 21. Capability-Health Methods

Capability-health methods are consequence-scaled rather than universal. Depending on the failure mode, continuing qualification may use end-to-end probes, source/consumer reconciliation, expected-volume or sequence checks, alternate-source comparison, physical or external confirmation, semantic sample testing, configuration and version monitoring, freshness checks, replay, adversarial test cases, or professional challenge.

The method must be capable of revealing the property on which qualification depends. A source-health check that confirms only that an endpoint returns HTTP 200 does not establish that the underlying data is current. A UI agent screenshot does not establish that a downstream transaction committed. A log writer reporting itself healthy does not establish that omitted events would be detectable.

Capability-health evidence should also preserve the historical interval of degradation. Current health can show that a path is now restored. It does not establish when it ceased to be healthy, when the loss first became observable, or which operations were affected. Those historical questions belong in the evidence needed by Volume 2.

## Part VII — Maintain, Restrict, Requalify, and Retire

### 22. Maintain, Restrict, or Degrade

When a qualified property degrades, the engineering response must preserve truthful capability state. Available responses include repair, a narrower operating envelope, a lower active mode, an alternate path, suspension, or explicitly authorized degraded operation.

The degradation record preserves the initiating evidence, affected requirements, actual restriction, authority, effective time, residual limitations, alternate-path status, and the historical degraded interval. If an alternate path is used, its own qualification and limitations apply; the organization may not inherit the properties of the failed path by substitution.

Operational continuity is not evidence continuity. A workflow may continue producing useful work after losing required independence, preventive timing, reconstruction, or effect observation. Volume 3 must make that distinction visible so that continuing operations do not silently retain assurance claims the path can no longer support.

Degraded operation remains an authorization decision constrained by qualification. Engineering identifies what remains technically supportable. Accountable authority decides whether continued operation within that reduced envelope is permitted.

### 23. Requalification After Material Change

Material change breaks silent inheritance. A favorable qualification cannot be assumed to survive a change to a property on which the qualification depended. Relevant changes include model, prompt, tool, schema, policy, authority, source, interface, transformation, workflow, environment, dependency, effect-observation path, reconstruction capability, and unexplained common-mode behavior.

Requalification is requirement-relative rather than indiscriminate. A change to a reporting template may not require retesting physical effect observation if no relevant dependency exists. A change to identity mapping may require requalification of every path whose correctness depends on that binding. The engineering burden is to show which qualified properties remain valid and which require renewed evidence.

No favorable result silently transfers across material change. Where requalification cannot be completed before operation must continue, the active mode or authority must reflect the unqualified condition rather than relying on prior evidence beyond its scope.

### 24. Retire, Replace, or Restore

Retirement is a legitimate engineering outcome. A capability may be obsolete, repeatedly failed, unsupported, superseded, unauthorized, or no longer worth maintaining relative to the governing claim. Volume 3 therefore treats retirement and replacement as normal lifecycle states rather than as evidence that the integration program failed.

Retirement preserves affected requirements, successor relationships, credential and access revocation, historical qualification state, retirement rationale, unresolved limitations, and any dependencies that must remain available for historical reconstruction. A retired path may still matter because past claim states depend on evidence created while it was active.

Restoration is not an administrative reversal. It requires adequate qualification evidence and accountable authorization. The historical degraded or retired interval remains intact even after the capability is restored.

Replacement also requires explicit relationship mapping. A new path does not inherit the old path's qualification merely because it serves the same business function. Its identity, dependencies, operating envelope, evidence properties, and challenge paths must be established independently.

### 25. Mission-Level Maintenance

Periodic maintenance asks whether the capability still serves the governing claim, whether the measure still measures the intended effect, whether the control still addresses the hazard that created it, and whether the path remains necessary. This keeps Volume 3 evolutionary rather than accumulative.

An integration can remain technically healthy while becoming organizationally obsolete. A control introduced after one incident can remain active long after the hazard has changed. A measure can continue producing clean numbers while the mission has shifted around it. A data source can remain available after a better, more direct evidence path becomes possible.

Volume 3 therefore treats removal, consolidation, and retirement as evidence-based engineering choices. Controls and paths retain provenance to the requirement, hazard, or seam that justified them so that the organization can determine when they should be retained, revised, restricted, replaced, or retired.

### 27. DRNT Realization and Implementation-Claim Discipline

DRNT is the originating architectural context, first intended realization, principal engineering inheritance, and initial environment in which Volume 3 requirements can be made concrete and tested. That relationship does not convert doctrine into implementation. Running-system claims remain controlled by current code, tests, receipts, and operational evidence.

The implementation-claim discipline distinguishes demonstrated primitive \[P\], specified mechanism \[S\], and synthetic or implementation hypothesis \[F\]. \[P\] means demonstrated only at the stated scale; it is never automatic proof at enterprise scale. \[S\] means specified in doctrine or design but not established as a running component. \[F\] means synthetic fixture behavior, enterprise realization hypothesis, scaling assumption, or other unproven implementation proposition.

Elsewhere in the manuscript, any statement that could reasonably be read as a running-system claim must remain consistent with this status. Governance language must not promote \[S\] or \[F\] mechanisms to \[P\] by prose.

The federal-scale fixture is useful as a synthetic stress test because it exposes design pressures and candidate patterns at scale. It is not evidence of deployment, federal compliance, or enterprise-scale performance. Its role is to challenge the architecture, not to prove implementation.

### 28. Federated and Heterogeneous Realization

Large organizations will commonly favor federated realization because claim-relevant evidence and authority are already distributed. Domains may need to preserve local systems, security, privacy, professional responsibility, records obligations, and operating context while still participating in a whole-of-organization assurance architecture.

Federation is therefore a common and likely pattern, not a universal prerequisite. A small organization may rationally implement many functions centrally. A large organization may use domain-specific stores and local control planes with a claim-relative integration layer. The architecture cares about the preserved functional distinctions and evidence properties, not the topology by which they are achieved.

Volume 3 explicitly rejects universal data-lake, universal identity, universal ontology, universal cloud, and universal connectivity requirements. A claim may require cross-domain binding without requiring the underlying domains to collapse into one schema or authority model.

Federation also reinforces a key principle: centralized awareness does not imply centralized authority. The architecture can assemble a connected operating picture while leaving decisions, professional responsibility, privacy controls, and local operational authority where they lawfully belong.

## Part IX — Limits, Interfaces, and Closing Architecture

### 29. Explicit Non-Ownership

Volume 3 does not define the organizational mission, define or authorize organizational claims, redefine the claim-relative system boundary, create authority, assign final organizational claim states, conduct conclusive self-evaluation of L3, convert qualification into authorization, convert authorization into evidence, make repeated operation legitimate, independently change the Declared Baseline, or authorize its own repairs or extensions.

It also does not require universal connectivity, one global identity, one ontology, one data lake, cloud migration, or maximum automation. It does not manufacture evidence that reality failed to preserve. Those limits are not missing features; they protect the three-volume allocation.

Volume 3 positively owns the engineering condition that makes L3 capable of being independently judged. It must keep the complete capability path qualifiable, observable, reconstructable, challengeable, maintainable, restrictable, revalidatable, and retireable relative to the governing claim and assurance burden.

### 30. Engineering Limits and Residual Conditions

Volume 3 does not promise complete enterprise visibility, perfect reconstruction, universal preventive intervention, universal connectivity, or zero residual risk. Some paths will remain indirect, late, unavailable, weakly independent, isolated, human-mediated, legally constrained, contractually constrained, or limited by legacy design.

Those limitations remain first-class engineering results. An indirect path does not disappear because direct evidence is preferable. A late path does not become preventive because intervention would have been useful. A weakly independent path does not become defensible because no better path exists. A legacy UI seam does not become an API by being automated.

Newly encountered problems are classified under the frozen change-control taxonomy. A finding that invalidates or materially contradicts the governing architecture is an Architecture Defect. A problem concerning assurance mechanisms, evidence, evaluation, learning, or evaluator governance is a Volume 2 Engineering Issue. A problem concerning evidence acquisition, interfaces, legacy systems, discovery, scalability, privacy, or as-operated connectivity is a Volume 3 Integration Issue. Cost, resources, latency, organizational resistance, technical feasibility, and deployment limitations are Implementation Constraints unless evidence demonstrates that they invalidate the architecture.

Implementation difficulty alone does not reopen the architecture. The correct response is to document, test, bound, and design around the constraint while keeping the limitation visible to the assurance process.

### 31. Worked Example — Legacy Maintenance Intervention

Assume Volume 2 requires independently defensible evidence that a hazardous equipment condition is present, requires an authorized intervention before a defined consequence window closes, and requires separate evidence that the hazardous condition was actually removed. The requirement is deliberately broader than an agent task. It asks whether the organization can detect a condition, route sufficient context to valid authority, act through the real maintenance system, and verify the resulting physical effect.

Discovery identifies four relevant elements. A modern sensor feed can observe the initiating condition. The maintenance application is a legacy system with no suitable API. An authorized professional must decide whether the condition warrants intervention under the governing procedure. The strongest post-work evidence is a physical inspection recorded by a separate field process. Discovery also identifies a human fallback procedure used when the legacy application is unavailable; because it is an alternate path, it must be separately designed, qualified, authorized, and reconstructable rather than inheriting the automated path’s properties.

During Design, L3 composes the complete path: sensor event to cross-system asset binding; condition package to the authorized professional; bounded UI/computer-use interaction with the legacy maintenance application; credential release only after the applicable authority is bound; dispatch and acknowledgment; work execution by the field organization; and post-work physical observation through a separately qualified inspection path. The design records that the legacy application's completion status cannot independently establish physical correction. The human fallback is designed as a separate intervention path with its own recipient and authority binding, delivery and acknowledgment method, timing allocation, action record, downstream reconciliation, and effect-observation dependency.

Qualification tests each property that the requirement depends upon. Sensor freshness and asset binding are verified. The professional receives the necessary condition history, applicable restriction, known uncertainty, and the time remaining inside the consequence window. The UI path is tested against the specific application version and credential scope. Downstream work-order creation is reconciled independently of the UI agent's own success signal. The physical inspection path is qualified as the effect-observation mechanism. The human fallback is separately qualified for the same intervention requirement under its defined operating conditions, including delivery, authority, timing, reconstruction, downstream acknowledgment, and effect observation. Its qualification records the limitations created by manual routing and the conditions under which it may substitute for the UI path.

Testing reveals two distinct timing propositions at one remote location. The intervention path can become effective inside the consequence window: the authorized work can be dispatched and the hazardous state can be constrained in time. The independently defensible physical effect-confirmation path, however, cannot return confirmation before that window closes. Volume 3 therefore does not label the intervention path Available too late for preventive control merely because effect evidence arrives later. The intervention requirement remains Available as required for the demonstrated preventive condition, while the separate effect-evidence requirement is Available with limitations for in-window confirmation and remains available for later effect verification. If testing instead showed that the complete effective-intervention path itself could not constrain the operation before irreversibility, that intervention requirement would be Available too late for preventive control.

Accountable Authorization permits the fully qualified automated path in the normal operating condition and separately authorizes the qualified human fallback under a bounded contingency rule for loss or restriction of the UI path. Activation binds the exact sensor version, identity mapping, context rules, professional role, UI workflow, credential scope, application version, effect-observation path, required sentinels, and the currently authorized fallback condition. The effective active mode is therefore the most restrictive result of technical qualification, accountable authorization, and current health.

At runtime, the Runtime Integration Envelope records the actual sensor event, asset mapping, context delivered and withheld, authority reference, UI interaction, credential release, dispatch, acknowledgment, field execution, and physical inspection. The work system's acknowledgment is preserved as execution evidence but is not promoted into proof that the hazardous condition was removed. The physical inspection remains the qualified effect-evidence path.

Months later, the legacy application changes its confirmation interface. The UI agent remains able to navigate the application, and the application continues responding, so there is no obvious outage. The change causes the automation to misread a screen state as successful submission. Reconciliation against downstream work-order records reveals a sequence gap: the UI path reports success while no corresponding work-order identity appears.

Continuing qualification classifies the event as silent interface drift, establishes the earliest defensible degraded interval, and determines that the UI path requires restriction and requalification. The authorized control mechanism applies that restriction and redirects the operation under the preauthorized contingency rule to the separately qualified human fallback; capability-health evaluation does not create the authority or itself perform Runtime Control. Operational continuity is preserved within the fallback’s own qualification, timing, reconstruction, and effect-observation limitations. The degraded interval, affected requirements, active-mode change, control decision, and alternate-path use remain historically visible.

Engineering corrects the interface binding and requalifies the affected path. Restoration requires evidence that the downstream reconciliation, target binding, and effect-observation chain again satisfy the requirement, followed by accountable authorization to restore the prior mode. The restored state does not rewrite the degraded interval.

Volume 3 then hands the evidence package to Volume 2: the requirement, qualification history, runtime envelopes, degraded interval, alternate-path status, effect evidence, and known limitations. Volume 3 does not assign the organizational claim state. Volume 2 independently determines what the evidence permits the organization to claim about the affected operations.

### 32. Conclusion

Volume 3 does not merely connect systems. It defines and maintains the bounded execution and enterprise-interaction layer through which governed claims and assurance requirements meet the enterprise that actually exists. Within DRNT, that layer is L3.

Its engineering object is the complete claim-relevant capability path. Its method is continuing qualification. Its discipline is to preserve the difference between technical capability, accountable authorization, active mode, evidence, and organizational claim state. Its operating rule is that a path remains useful only to the extent that the organization can continue to observe, challenge, maintain, restrict, requalify, and reconstruct the properties on which its qualification depends.

The architecture therefore accepts heterogeneous reality rather than requiring the enterprise to rebuild itself around AI. APIs, databases, UI agents, professional judgment, physical observation, isolated systems, external organizations, and human workarounds can all participate when they are appropriately bounded and qualified. Some paths will remain late, indirect, unavailable, or weakly independent. Those conditions constrain capability; they do not disappear from the record.

Volume 3 also refuses self-certification. L3 may perform and observe work, but it does not define its own mission, create its own authority, certify its own evidence, assign the organizational claim state, or authorize changes to the model under which it operates. Volume 2 independently determines what the resulting evidence permits the organization to claim.

The three-volume sequence is therefore complete in function. Volume 1 defines reality: the claim-relevant organizational system and authority against which the organization is governed. Volume 2 evaluates reality: what happened, what the evidence supports, and whether the operating model remains appropriate. Volume 3 connects reality: how those requirements reach the heterogeneous as-built and as-operated enterprise and remain qualified as that enterprise changes.

Define reality. Evaluate reality. Connect reality.
